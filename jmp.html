Local Queens and Princesses: The Impact of Female Dynasts on Provision of Public Goods

Aqila Putri · This version: October 2026

Download the paper (PDF) All research

Female representation in politics remains limited, and the women who do reach office frequently come from political dynasties, in part because of the entry barriers they face. The effects of female and dynastic politicians on their constituents vary with the setting, so what happens when a female dynast is elected is an empirical question. I study this question in the context of Indonesia, where district governments hold statutory responsibility for basic services. I combine district elections from 2005 to 2020 with eight waves of a national village census, and I estimate the effect with a heterogeneity-robust event study estimator. Electing a female dynast raises an index of public infrastructure by 0.075 of a village standard deviation, and household infrastructure and education carry the gain. The gender contrast is about two and a half times the dynastic one. The effect is largest where her family never held the office and its network is wider and older.
