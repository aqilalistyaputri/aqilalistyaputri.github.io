Selected Working Papers

Does Rent Beget Rent? Natural Resource Boom and Political Selection

Job market paper

This paper asks whether economic rents entrench political dynasties. Using Indonesia's nickel boom and an original dataset of district-level political dynasties covering 2005–2018, I use an event-study design that exploits the staggered timing of nickel permit openings. Nickel openings raise the share of dynastic politicians selected into district office by roughly six percentage points over the medium term, an effect driven primarily by the persistence of incumbent dynasties rather than the entry of new ones.

Local Queens and Princesses: The Impact of Female Dynasts on Provision of Public Goods

When a woman from a political dynasty takes local office, two forces pull in opposite directions: the documented benefits of female political representation and the documented costs of dynastic rule. Combining Indonesian national survey data with close-election variation in regional races, I estimate the net effect on public goods provision. Female dynasts have small but positive effects on infrastructure, concentrated in domains associated with women's priorities.
