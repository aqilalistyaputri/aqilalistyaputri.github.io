Local Queens and Princesses: The Impact of Female Dynasts on Provision of Public Goods

Female representation in politics remains limited despite extensive efforts to bolster it. However, female politicians frequently emerge from political dynasties, influenced by the considerable entry barriers women face in politics. Consequently, two opposing forces come into play when a female dynast is elected to office: female politicians have been shown to be beneficial for their constituents, especially their female constituents, but dynasts tend to have negative effects. This study investigates which force dominates using the context of Indonesia. Combining district elections from 2005 to 2020 with eight waves of a national village census, I find that electing a female dynast raises an index of public infrastructure by 0.07 of a village standard deviation. When I separate the two characteristics, the gain follows her gender, while the dynastic tie is smaller but does not work against it. The effect is largest in districts where her family had never held the executive office before, and smallest where a relative held it with terms in between.

Does Rent Beget Rent? Natural Resource Boom and Political Selection

How do economic shocks shape the entrenchment of political dynasties? I study Indonesia’s nickel boom—a large, location-specific rent shock driven by electric-vehicle battery demand and Chinese downstream investment—and its effect on the election of dynastic politicians in district-level office. Using an original dataset on Indonesian dynasties from 2005 to 2018 and an event-study design that exploits the staggered timing of a district’s first nickel permit, I show that a nickel opening is followed by a medium-run rise in dynastic selection on the order of six percentage points. The effect runs through dynastic persistence—the election of dynastic descendants. Falsification tests using coal and gold permits produce no analogous effect. Resource booms can entrench the political concentrations that already exist in the places that hold them.
